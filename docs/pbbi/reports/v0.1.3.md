# PBBI clause extraction — v0.1.3 (current `stable`)

**Date:** 2026-10-17 (evaluation), 2026-10-21/25 (client report for Bank Indonesia) · **Model:** `scalejade/pbbi-clause-extraction-lora` tag `v0.1.3` = `stable` (commit `63773a48`) on base `scalejade/qwen-sea-lion-v4.5-27b-pbbi` rev `8d32a4b9` · **Test:** the same 17 contracts as v0.1.2 (2,074 clauses; `test.jsonl` byte-identical) · **Temperature:** 0 for every run here

This file merges the three v0.1.3 reports (vs v0.1.2, vs gpt-4.1-mini, client report EN/ID). Two metrics appear:
- **Jaccard average (official, used with BI and in the app RFC):** every labelled clause is paired one to one with its most similar model clause, closest pairs first; score = Jaccard over word sets, no threshold, unmatched = 0. Recall = total score ÷ labelled clauses; precision = total score ÷ returned clauses.
- **Strict (≥ 0.8):** a clause counts as found only if it shares ≥ 80% of its words with a labelled clause. Used in the internal reports of 17 Oct.

## 4. What the results show

1. **gpt-4.1-mini returns less than half of the clauses** (929 for 2,074) and nearly nothing on BJB (4/110), SINARMAS (4/49), OCBC KK (19/233), SEABANK (42/358). Every reply ended with `stop`, well under its 32,768-token limit. At t=0 it stops even earlier than at 1.0 (strict F1 0.391 vs 0.470).
2. **v0.1.3 follows BI's level of detail** (0.84× the labelled count) and its lead is largest on long contracts (≥ 200 clauses: 67 vs 26 recall).
3. **More data helped only the types that were added**: DBS +0.112, BJB +0.057 (long MB). Types with no new data (UE, Payment Gateway) slipped slightly.
4. **OCBC IB did not move** (0.364 → 0.356) despite two more IB contracts in training. The labels group definitions in ranges and the model gives one clause per term; more data cannot fix a rule the labels contradict. The deeper analysis of the four losses (copied numbering, unrepeated definition preamble, split tables, merged sub-items) is in [v0.1.4.md](v0.1.4.md).
5. **Temperature 0 is worth keeping for repeatability**, not for F1.

## 5. Data and split (client report, chapter 2)

60 contracts from 41 providers, 9 products, 6,890 labelled clauses (BI's Excel: No, No. Pasal, Topik Klausa, Bunyi Klausa). Categorised from BI's PB Mainsheet in four layers: provider flags (Utama, BPD, Multinational, Swasta Nasional, BUMN, LSB; counts 43/15/12/22/8/3) → product (9) → provider type (7) → category = type × product (29). All 60 were checked against the mainsheet.

Split rules: every one of the 29 categories has a training contract; 17 categories are also tested (the other 12 have only one or two contracts); the test group never changes. Moves made: CIMB UE Server (val → train) and OCBC KD, MANDIRI KD (test → train) because their category had no training contract; BRI UE Chip (train → test) so card-based e-money is tested; the 8 v0.1.3 moves above.

## 8. Clause-by-clause workbook format (reference for the app export, RFC §5.4)

The comparison workbook (built by `preparation/compare_runs.py`) quotes the contracts, so it is never committed. Its format, which the app's Excel export should follow:

| Sheet | Columns / content |
|---|---|
| **Ringkasan** | Per model: model, temperature, labelled clauses, returned, total similarity score, recall, precision, F1, clauses with high / medium / low-or-missing score, returned ÷ labelled, valid JSON, finished on its own, output tokens, longest contract (min); then how to read the sheets and how the score is computed |
| **Per kontrak** | No · Kontrak · Penyelenggara · Produk · Klausa (rincian BI) · per model: dikembalikan, total skor, recall, precision, Skor F1, skor ≥ 0,80, menit · F1 lebih tinggi |
| **Perbandingan klausa** | One row per BI clause: Kontrak · No · No. Pasal (BI) · Topik Klausa (BI) · Bunyi Klausa (BI) · per model: status, Kemiripan, No. Pasal, Topik Klausa, Bunyi Klausa. After each contract, model clauses with no partner |
| **<model> (mentah)** | Kontrak · # · No. Pasal · Topik Klausa · Bunyi Klausa · Kemiripan dengan pasangan BI · Status |

Status bands (for filtering only; they do not change R/P/F1): **Skor tinggi** ≥ 0.80 (green) · **Skor sedang** 0.50–0.79 (yellow) · **Skor rendah** < 0.50 / **Tidak ditemukan** (red) · **Tanpa pasangan** (orange; counts in the precision denominator).

Scoring steps: lower-case, keep letters, digits and spaces, split into a set of unique words → Jaccard = shared ÷ union → pair closest first, one to one, no threshold → recall, precision, F1 over the 17-contract totals (not averaged per contract). Example: BI "Bank adalah PT Bank Pembangunan Daerah Jawa Barat dan Banten, Tbk." (10 unique words) vs model "2. Bank adalah …" (11, extra "2") → 10 ÷ 11 = 0.91.

## 9. Caveats and raw data

- 17 test contracts; one contract moves F1 by about 0.01, so v0.1.3's +0.007 over v0.1.2 is within one contract's swing.
- Validation is 4 contracts: enough to pick the checkpoint, noisy as a loss.
- Six banks have a different product in train than in test (BCA, JATENG, MANDIRI, OCBC, SINARMAS, SMBC); BCA KD and JATENG KD scored the same as before.
- Raw runs (gitignored, quote the contracts): `preparation/data/scores/test-v0.1.3-t0/`, `test-v0.1.2-t0/`, `test-gpt-4.1-mini-t0/`, `test-gpt-4.1-mini/` (t=1.0). Re-run: `make evaluate ARGS="--model pbbi-clause-extraction --temperature 0"`. Rebuild the workbook:
